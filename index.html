    About SARITA
    
        
    सरिता (saritā) means “river”. SAR-based Inundation Tracking & Analytics.
    
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    
        
    The problem
    
        
    Bihar is India's most flood-prone state. Every monsoon the Kosi, the river known as the “Sorrow of Bihar”, and its neighbours flood North Bihar.
          In late September 2024 heavy rain in Nepal pushed the Kosi barrage at Birpur to its highest discharge in decades, and embankments breached in several districts.
    
        
    Relief agencies need to know where the water is, how much land and how many people are affected, and how this changes from day to day.
          Optical satellites cannot see the ground through monsoon cloud. Synthetic-aperture radar (SAR) can: Sentinel-1 images the ground day and night through cloud, and calm water shows up dark because it reflects the radar pulse away from the sensor.
    
        
    What SARITA does
    
        
      
          
    • A U-Net with an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-34 encoder segments water in Sentinel-1 VV/VH backscatter. It is trained on the hand-labelled Sen1Floods11 benchmark and compared with classical thresholding and machine-learning baselines.
      • 
          
    • Uncertainty: test-time augmentation, MC dropout, calibration (ECE, temperature scaling) and risk–coverage analysis show when the model should not be trusted.
      • 
          
    • Case study: whole-scene inference over the Kosi–Mithila region (≈21,500 km²) on every Sentinel-1 pass from 15 September to 2 November 2024. Flooding is measured against the pre-flood image and JRC permanent water.
      • 
          
    • Impact: flooded cropland and built-up area from ESA WorldCover and people exposed from WorldPop, aggregated per district. English/Hindi bulletins are generated offline.
      • 
        
    
        
    All estimates on this dashboard are satellite-based and meant for planning. They are not official figures.
